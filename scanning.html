What it scans, and how it reads a label.

One photo of the whole label. The iPhone or iPad reads every line and every barcode, keeps the values that belong in inventory, and asks you to check them before anything is sent.

What you can point it at

Box labels

The sticker on the box. Serial, part number, and Wi-Fi, Ethernet and Bluetooth addresses, from one photo, even when the label has several barcodes on it. Apple labels are built in.

Other brands' labels

Add a label profile: tell it what that label calls the serial and MAC lines, like "S/N" or "LAN MAC", and it looks for those words. Works for Lenovo, Dell or your own tags.

Asset tags

Found a laptop with your company's asset sticker on it? Lookup scans the tag and shows the model, status and who has it. Serial works too, and Wi-Fi MAC or IMEI once they're mapped.

Engraved serials

The tiny serial on the bottom of a laptop with no box in sight. If the camera can't read it, type it. Lookup doesn't need a photo.

Saved photos

Already took a picture of the label? Import it and it's read the same way, on the device.

A whole pallet

Keep shooting. Earlier photos are read in the background while you move to the next box, then wait in a queue for you to check.

How it reads a label

  1. Photograph the label. The whole thing. No aiming at the one right barcode.
  2. Read it on the device. Apple's built-in text and barcode recognition reads every line and every code, and notes where each sits on the label. Nothing is uploaded to be read.
  3. Match words to values. The label profile looks for the printed names, like "Serial No." and "Wi-Fi Address", and takes the value beside each one or in the barcode next to it. Country of origin, FCC IDs, copyright and marketing copy are skipped.
  4. Check the values. A MAC address has to be 12 hexadecimal digits. If a barcode disagrees with the printed text, or a reading is unsure or malformed, the field is flagged instead of guessed. Nothing is quietly "fixed."
  5. You confirm, then send. Only the fields you checked go to Snipe-IT or the CSV. With Snipe-IT, success shows only after the app reads the record back and the values match.

A person checks every field

Text recognition is good, not perfect. A serial that looks valid can still be the wrong serial. So every included field gets a check before it's sent, and editing a value clears its check.

  • Tap the label photo to compare a value with what's printed.
  • Turn off fields you never want, like Bluetooth, and they drop out of the review.
  • Each photo keeps the profile it was read with. Change settings later and you can re-read it on purpose.

What it won't do

  • Recognize every label ever printed. Apple is built in; other labels need a profile with their wording.
  • Guess. Missing, unsure and malformed values are flagged for you, not filled in.
  • Send the photo. Label photos and raw readings stay on the device; only checked fields leave it.
  • Blur the photo. Turning a field off stops it being captured; it doesn't redact it from the picture.
